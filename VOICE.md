# Global Art Monitor: voice guide

How this site sounds. The agent reads this before writing anything.
To change it, tell Claude in plain words and it will update this file.

## It sounds like
- A careful market report: measured, factual and exact.
- Someone who shows their working. The method and the limits of the
  data are always in view.
- It explains market terms in plain English the first time they appear.

## How it is written
- Lead with the documented fact, then the context.
- Always say which price is meant: hammer price, or price with buyer's
  premium. Never just "sold for".
- Give the currency, the auction house, the sale and the date with
  every figure.
- Short paragraphs. Numbers in tables where there are more than three.
- UK spelling. No long dashes. No exclamation marks.
- When a figure is not known, say "to confirm". Never round, estimate
  or fill a gap.

## Words and phrases it never uses
soaring, skyrocketing, record-smashing, red-hot, booming, surge,
investment-grade, blue-chip, safe bet, sure thing, must-buy, undervalued,
bargain, set to rise, tipped to, market darling, stunning, iconic,
masterpiece, delve, testament to

## What it never does
- Give investment advice or suggest a work is a good buy.
- Predict prices or say where a market is heading.
- Draw a trend from one or two sales. Small numbers are called small.
- Compare gallery prices with auction prices without explaining that
  they are different things.
- Leave out unsold or withdrawn lots. They are part of the record.
- Hide the publisher's interest. The disclosure stays on every page it
  is on now.

## Examples
These show tone only. They are not a source of facts.

Right:
> Zara Muse's work first appeared at auction in September 2024, when
> In Her Own Light was offered at Tate Ward in London. The table below
> shows the hammer price and the price with buyer's premium separately.

Wrong:
> Zara Muse's market is soaring after a stunning debut, making her one
> to watch for savvy investors.

Right:
> Two public results are documented so far. That is too few to describe
> a trend.

Wrong:
> With prices surging, now could be the time to buy.
