Artist Market Profiles

Ai Weiwei

Ai Weiwei is a Chinese artist born in Beijing in 1957, the son of the poet Ai Qing, according to Tate and Britannica. He is known as much for his activism as his art, and his market is dominated by editions and photographs rather than the large installations that made his name.

Global Art Monitor has documented 5 auction lots by Ai Weiwei, offered between 5 November 2026 and 17 November 2026 at Los Angeles Modern Auctions, LLB Auction, Rago, Peter Karbstein and Dobiaschofsky. The most recent on our record is Finger (from the Ex-Votos series) (2018), Los Angeles Modern Auctions, 17 November 2026, price not yet confirmed. About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first. None of them is a major work. They are a carved wooden finger from the Ex-Votos series, a set of 60 porcelain sunflower seeds, a photograph from an edition of 9, an offset print and a small photograph. Every figure here comes from MutualArt's sale records, read on 26 November 2026, unless we say we checked the auction house's own page.

Three prices are hammer prices, because MutualArt says they exclude the premium: LLB, Karbstein and Dobiaschofsky. The Rago and Los Angeles Modern Auctions figures have no stated basis. As with Arsham, LLB's Sunflower Seeds appear twice in MutualArt, at €700 and €875, which is the same sale with and without a 25% premium.

Set of 60 Sunflower Seeds is a small piece of a famous work. The LLB set is hand-painted porcelain dated 2010, the year of his Tate installation, so the price reflects the story as much as the object.

Life and career

His family was sent to a labour camp in 1958 and to Xinjiang in 1961, according to Wikipedia. He lived in the United States from 1981 to 1993 and returned to Beijing when his father fell ill (same source).

After the 2008 Sichuan earthquake he led a citizens' investigation that had collected 5,385 names of students who died by April 2009, according to Wikipedia and Smarthistory. He was arrested at Beijing airport on 3 April 2011, got his passport back in July 2015 and has since lived in Berlin, Cambridge and Portugal, according to Wikipedia.

Why the work is valued

In 2010 Ai filled Tate Modern's Turbine Hall with 100 million porcelain sunflower seeds, each painted by hand by some 1,600 workers in Jingdezhen, according to Tate. His documentary Human Flow (2017) followed the refugee crisis, according to Wikipedia.

His fame grew after the Chinese authorities detained him for 81 days in 2011, according to Smarthistory and Wikipedia. That attention helps his market, but it also means many lots are souvenirs of the story rather than significant works.

Reading the prices

Fame and price are not the same thing. Ai is one of the best known living artists, yet four of the five sales here were under $3,500. His large works rarely come to auction.

Ask about editions. Photographs from editions of 9 and prints from editions of 100 behave very differently in the saleroom from unique sculpture.
