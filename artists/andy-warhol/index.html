Andy Warhol

Andy Warhol was an American artist, born Andrew Warhola in Pittsburgh on 6 August 1928, who died in New York on 22 February 1987. He worked as a commercial illustrator in New York in the 1950s, then made his name in the early 1960s with paintings of soup cans, film stars and news photographs, most of them printed onto canvas by photo-silkscreen.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales of unique works we could document, newest first. Four of them were sold in one night, 16 November 2026, at Sotheby's New York, and one three days later at Christie's.

Christie's lists Campbell's Soup Can (Turkey Noodle) (1962), from the collection of Antonio Homem Sonnabend, as sold on 19 November 2026 for US$9,026,000 against an estimate of US$6 million to 8 million. The Art Newspaper gives a hammer price of US$7.3 million. Christie's New York rates from September 2026 turn that hammer into exactly US$9,026,000, so the two sources agree. We have seen Christie's results listing for this lot but not the full lot page, so its lot number and medium are still to confirm.

Sotheby's shows its results only to signed-in users, so the four Sotheby's prices rest on press reports, while the lot numbers, sizes, media and estimates come from Sotheby's own lot pages. The 48 inch Flowers (lot 112), one of seven paintings of that size according to Sotheby's, made a hammer price of US$15.2 million and US$18.2 million with fees, according to The Art Newspaper. Sotheby's 2026 New York rates turn that hammer into US$18,160,000, shown as our calculation. Artdaily reports the 24 inch Flowers of 1964 (lot 101, from the collection of Beverly Grossman) at US$6.1 million and Dolly Parton (lot 124) at US$4.1 million, both well above their high estimates, but gives no hammer prices, so both totals stay to confirm.

The Scream (After Munch) (1984), lot 7 of the Baum collection sale earlier the same evening, is reported at US$7.9 million with fees by The Art Newspaper and by Luxury.it. The Art Newspaper also gives a hammer price of US$6.8 million, but Sotheby's published rates would turn that into about US$8.4 million, not US$7.9 million. Because the figures do not agree, we leave both the hammer and the total to confirm. Sotheby's lot page marks the work as guaranteed with irrevocable bids.

Two lots did not sell. Sotheby's lot page for Double Liz (lot 126) shows it as withdrawn from the 16 November sale, and The Art Newspaper reports that Christie's withdrew a Warhol self portrait, estimated at US$14 million to 18 million, from its 20th Century Evening Sale on 17 November.

Works on paper and prints are tracked separately, following our guide on unique works and editions. On 19 November Christie's sold The Kiss (Bela Lugosi) (1963), silkscreen ink on paper, for US$4,634,000 against US$3 million to 5 million. Phillips' own lot page shows Ten Portraits of Jews of the Twentieth Century (1980), a set of ten screenprints numbered 103 from an edition of 200, sold for US$1,096,500 on 17 November, against US$400,000 to 600,000. MyArtBroker reports its hammer at US$850,000 and a complete set of Endangered Species (1983) hammered at its US$2.5 million low estimate at Sotheby's on 16 November.

Why the work is valued

The Museum of Modern Art in New York owns Campbell's Soup Cans (1962), the 32 canvases first shown at the Ferus Gallery in Los Angeles in July 1962, and Tate in London owns Marilyn Diptych (1962). The Andy Warhol Museum in Pittsburgh is described as the largest museum in the United States devoted to a single artist.

His market is one of the deepest of any post-war artist. Christie's own search lists 5,892 sold lots by him, from paintings and drawings to prints and photographs. So a Warhol price only means something when it is compared with the same kind of work: a unique canvas, a print from an edition of 250 and a Polaroid sit in different markets.
