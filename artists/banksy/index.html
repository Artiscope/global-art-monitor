Banksy

Banksy is an anonymous artist who began as a teenage graffiti writer in Bristol in the 1990s. He works mainly in spray paint through hand-cut stencils, on walls and on canvas, and also makes screenprint editions and sculptural pieces from found objects.

About the artist

The table below lists the five most recent public sales of unique works that we could document, all from autumn 2026. The prices for Angel and the 2001 Laugh Now are stated with buyer's premium on the Bonhams lot pages. The same pages publish Bonhams' premium rates, and working back from them gives hammer prices of exactly US$1,100,000 for Angel and GBP 250,000 for Laugh Now, shown as our calculation. Phillips' lot page for Keep Ou T gives "Sold For £1,612,500" without naming the basis; we show it as the price with premium. Bonhams no longer shows estimates once a sale has closed, so the estimates for both Bonhams lots come from Banksy Explained, a tracking site.

Two prices come from reports rather than the auction houses, because Heritage Auctions and Sotheby's show their results only to signed-in users. Artdaily and Antiques and The Arts Weekly report that Rat & Heart brought US$387,500 at Heritage on 10 November 2026, and Banksy Explained gives the same figure against an estimate of US$300,000 to 500,000. None of them says whether the price includes the premium, so the hammer price stays to confirm. Banksy Explained reports GBP 256,000 for the 2002 Laugh Now at Sotheby's London on 16 October 2026. Sotheby's has charged 28% on the first £1.5 million since February 2026, according to the Antiques Trade Gazette, and GBP 256,000 works back to a hammer price of exactly GBP 200,000, so we treat it as the price with premium and show the hammer as our calculation.

Unsold lots are part of the record. Sotheby's withdrew a signed Flower Thrower Triptych (2019, VIP edition 7/15) from its November 2026 New York sale. Grannies (2006), offered at Sotheby's London on 15 October 2026 with an estimate of GBP 1.5 million to 2 million, and Rat Clock with Headset (2019), offered at Sotheby's New York on 7 October 2026, are reported as unsold. Sotheby's pages show no result for either.

Prints and other editions are tracked separately, because an edition of hundreds is not comparable with a unique canvas. They will be added once we have checked each result with the auction house.

Life and career

Banksy has never publicly confirmed his identity. The documented career starts in Bristol in the 1990s, where he wrote graffiti as a teenager before moving to London, according to the Jordan Schnitzer Museum of Art. Canvases followed early: Laugh Now was painted on canvas in 2001 and bought from the artist the same year, according to Bonhams.

Two shows mark the move from the street to institutions. Barely Legal in Los Angeles in 2006 put large canvases such as Angel in front of American collectors. In 2009 Banksy Versus Bristol Museum filled the city museum with about 100 works, free to enter, from June to 31 August, as reported by the Mail & Guardian.

Why the work is valued

Banksy built his reputation mostly outside museums, through work on public walls. His best known moment at auction is Love is in the Bin. As Girl with Balloon it shredded itself after the hammer fell at £1m at a Sotheby's sale in 2018. Resold at Sotheby's London on 14 October 2021, it made a hammer price of £16m and £18.6m with fees, as reported by The Art Newspaper.

Authentication matters a great deal for his market. His office, Pest Control, says it is the only body authorised to authenticate his work, and its terms say it does not authenticate pieces made in, or taken from, public spaces. Our guide to provenance and authentication explains what that means for buyers.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Girl with Balloon (2002)

Stencil mural; later canvases and prints

First painted near Waterloo Bridge in London. A framed version shredded itself at Sotheby's in 2018 straight after the hammer fell at £1m, and as Love is in the Bin it sold again in 2021 for £18.6m with fees. The image also exists as signed and unsigned screenprints, which sell at far lower levels and are a separate market.

Laugh Now (2001 and 2002)

Acrylic and spray paint on canvas

A chimpanzee wearing a sandwich board. Two canvases of this image sold in London in October 2026, one at Bonhams and one at Sotheby's, which makes them a rare like-for-like pair. They appear in the table above.

Keep Ou T (2019)

Reclaimed airport customs arch, wood, metal and spray paint

A found object, reworked. Sculptural pieces like this are rarer at auction than canvases, so a single result tells you little about the next one.

Reading the prices

Start with what the object is. A unique canvas, a found object, a signed screenprint and an unsigned screenprint are four different markets that happen to share a name. Our table lists unique works only.

Then check the paperwork. Every recent lot we checked came with a Pest Control certificate. Works taken from walls are a separate case, because Pest Control says it does not authenticate them.
