Damien Hirst

Damien Hirst was born in Bristol in 1965 and lives and works between London and Gloucestershire, according to White Cube. He won the Turner Prize in 1995. He is known for animals preserved in formaldehyde, medicine cabinets, spot and spin paintings, and a very large output of prints.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first, all at Phillips and all confirmed on Phillips' lot pages. Only one is a unique work: Potassium Chloride ACS Reagent (2004), a spot painting in household gloss, which sold in London on 17 October 2026 for £154,800 with premium, just above its high estimate.

The other four are prints. They include The Wonder of You (2015), a set of six etchings numbered 31 from an edition of 55, at US$77,400, and The Currency Unique Print H11-495 (2022), a giclée print from a run of 1,000 variants, at US$6,192.

Two unique works offered in October show no price on their lot pages: Beautiful, Beautiful George Michael Love Painting (2017), a large spin painting estimated at £120,000 to 180,000 in London on 17 October, and Beautiful Paper Skull Spin AL (2013), estimated at €30,000 to 50,000 in Paris on 21 October. We cannot tell from the pages whether they failed to sell, so we record them here rather than in the table.

Life and career

White Cube lists the main museum shows: Astrup Fearnley Museet, Oslo (2005), the Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam (2008), Tate Modern (2012), and Treasures from the Wreck of the Unbelievable in Venice in 2017, a ten-year project of almost 190 works shown across Palazzo Grassi and Punta della Dogana.

Recent shows include the Albertina Modern in Vienna in 2025 and the National Museum of Modern and Contemporary Art in Seoul, from 20 March to 28 June 2026.

Why the work is valued

Mother and Child (Divided), made for the 1993 Venice Biennale and shown at the Turner Prize in 1995, is in the collection of the Astrup Fearnley Museet in Oslo. Tate owns Pharmacy (1992) and The Acquired Inability to Escape (1991), and held a retrospective in 2012. White Cube counts more than 90 solo exhibitions since 1987.

He makes a great deal of work, much of it in editions. That is why his auction record is dominated by prints, and why a result for one spot painting tells you little about another.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Mother and Child (Divided) (1993)

Cow and calf, bisected, in formaldehyde solution, in four glass tanks. Astrup Fearnley Museet, Oslo

The centre of his 1995 Turner Prize show, according to Tate, and first made for the Venice Biennale in 1993.

Pharmacy (1992)

Installation. Tate

Tate's record lists medicine packaging, apothecary bottles, desks and an insect-o-cutor among its materials. White Cube links it to his interest in pharmaceuticals and science.

Spot paintings

Household gloss on canvas, and screenprints

Rows of coloured spots, often titled after chemicals. Potassium Chloride ACS Reagent, sold in October 2026, is one. Gold Gift Spot, sold in September, is a screenprint from an edition of 150.

Reading the prices

Four of the five results in the table are for editions, ranging from £4,902 to US$77,400. They should not be read against the unique spot painting.

Two unique works offered in October show no price. Unsold lots are part of the record, but without confirmation we do not count them either way.

These figures are a record of past public sales. They are not a valuation and they are not advice. For how to read them, start with what an auction result actually tells collectors.
