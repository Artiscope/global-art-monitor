David Shrigley

David Shrigley was born in Macclesfield in 1968 and lives in Brighton, according to the Mayor of London's Fourth Plinth programme. His drawings, animations, sculptures and prints are deliberately crude and very funny, and he was shortlisted for the Turner Prize in 2013.

Global Art Monitor has documented 5 auction lots by David Shrigley, offered between 16 September 2026 and 29 October 2026 at Phillips and Tate Ward. The most recent on our record is Leap Into The Void (2023), Tate Ward, 29 October 2026, sold for £720 with buyer's premium. Source: Tate Ward lot page.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first. All five are screenprints, which is typical of his auction record: Tate Ward alone lists more than 700 past Shrigley lots. Prices come from the lot pages of Phillips and Tate Ward and include the buyer's premium.

The range is wide for works of the same size and edition. In Phillips' online editions sale that closed on 22 September 2026, My Rampage is Over (2019), numbered 81 of 125, sold for £20,640 against an estimate of £5,000 to 7,000, while Thank You for Keeping Still (2023), also from an edition of 125, sold for £1,032. Tate Ward's sales in September and October sold others for a few hundred pounds, and several Shrigley lots in its October sale show no result.

Unique works appear less often. The most recent we found is an untitled acrylic on paper of 2017, which sold for £6,096 in Phillips' London online sale that closed on 3 July 2025.

Life and career

The Fourth Plinth programme describes work that draws on the British tradition of satire. Tate's Turner Prize text speaks of black humour and macabre intelligence. Tate Ward's catalogue notes that he studied at Glasgow School of Art.

Alongside drawing he has made animations, artist's books and performance. Pass the Spoon was performed at Tramway in Glasgow and the Southbank Centre in London in 2011 and 2012. He had a solo show at the Pinakothek der Moderne in Munich in 2014.

Why the work is valued

He was shortlisted for the Turner Prize in 2013 for his Hayward Gallery show Brain Activity, and the prize exhibition in Derry~Londonderry included his installation Life Model (2012). In 2016 his seven metre bronze thumbs-up, Really Good, stood on the Fourth Plinth in Trafalgar Square. The National Gallery of Victoria in Melbourne gave him his first major Australian survey, Life and Life Drawing, in 2014 to 2015.

Most of the Shrigleys that reach auction are open or large editions, sold for hundreds or low thousands of pounds. The price of a print depends mostly on the image and on how many were made.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Really Good (2016)

Bronze, about 7 metres high. Fourth Plinth, Trafalgar Square

A hand giving a thumbs-up, with the thumb much too long. It was cast with the same dark patina as the other statues in the square, and the programme says he meant the gesture to work as a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Life Model (2012)

Installation

Shown in the Turner Prize exhibition in Derry~Londonderry in 2013, the year he was shortlisted.

Untitled (Problem) (2014)

Synthetic polymer paint on paper, 153 x 111 cm

Shown at the National Gallery of Victoria in Life and Life Drawing, lent by Stephen Friedman Gallery. Hand-lettered words and a crude image, the format most of his prints follow.

Reading the prices

Every result in the table is for an editioned print. The gap between £1,032 and £20,640 for two screenprints of the same size and edition is a reminder that each image has its own following.

Prints in open editions, with no fixed number made, have the least resale value, and several sold at Tate Ward for £212 to £375.
