Artist Market Profiles

El Anatsui

El Anatsui is a Ghanaian sculptor, born in Anyako in 1944, who has taught and worked in Nsukka, Nigeria, since 1975. He is best known for large wall hangings made from flattened aluminium bottle caps joined with copper wire. He received the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the Venice Biennale in 2015.

About the artist

Only two public auction lots from 2025 and 2026 could be documented, both at Sotheby's in London. G6 (2023) was offered on 4 March 2026 in a group of works donated by artists to support the Royal Academy, against an estimate of GBP 800,000 to 1.2 million. Artdaily reports GBP 742,400 (US$995,000), without naming the basis. Sotheby's shows the result only to signed-in users. Sotheby's rate since February 2026 is 28% on the first £1.5 million, and GBP 742,400 works back to a hammer price of exactly GBP 580,000, so we treat it as the price with premium and show the hammer as our calculation.

Hesitant Rivers (2012) closed on 16 October 2025 with the same estimate. Sotheby's shows the result only to signed-in users and we found no published report of it. We also tried Artnet and MutualArt, which refused automated visits, and auction summaries for that week. Whether it sold, and for how much, is still to confirm.

Two results are too few to describe his market. Artnet lists further lots offered in 2026, but shows their prices only to subscribers. The widely reported US$2.2 million sale of LuwVor I at Frieze New York in May 2026 was a gallery sale, not an auction, and is not in the table.

Life and career

Anatsui studied at the College of Art at the Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology in Kumasi from 1965 to 1968, then taught sculpture at the University of Nigeria in Nsukka for more than four decades, according to The Art Newspaper. Early work was carved wood and clay; a Danish residency produced driftwood sculptures such as Akua's Surviving Children (1996), according to Studio International.

The bottle-cap hangings brought international attention in the 2000s. October Gallery in London showed Man's Cloth and Woman's Cloth in 2002, and the British Museum acquired them. By 2007 such works were in the de Young Museum, the Centre Pompidou and the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and that year he showed Dusasa I and II at the Venice Biennale, according to Studio International.

Why the work is valued

El Anatsui received the Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement at the Venice Biennale in 2015 and the Praemium Imperiale in 2017. In October 2023 Tate Modern unveiled his Hyundai Commission, Behind the Red Moon, in the Turbine Hall, which Tate described as his largest work to date. Tate lists his work in the collections of the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the British Museum, the Centre Pompidou and the Smithsonian's National Museum of African Art.

We could document only two auction lots from 2025 and 2026, so his museum record says far more about his standing than his auction record does.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Man's Cloth and Woman's Cloth (2002)

Aluminium bottle caps and copper wire

Shown at October Gallery in London and bought by the British Museum. An early example of a museum acquiring the hangings soon after they were made.

Behind the Red Moon (2023)

Three-part installation, Tate Modern Turbine Hall

The Hyundai Commission, in three acts: The Red Moon, The World and The Wall. Works on this scale are made for institutions and do not come to auction.

Hesitant Rivers (2012)

Aluminium bottle caps and copper wire

A tall hanging with bands of colour running down it. Hangings of this kind are what reaches the saleroom, and size matters a great deal when comparing them.

In the artist's words

Reading the prices

Anatsui's hangings have no fixed shape. He lets each installer drape them differently, so a catalogue's dimensions describe one hanging, not the work for all time. Check whether a lot is a full wall hanging, a smaller relief or a wooden sculpture before comparing prices.
