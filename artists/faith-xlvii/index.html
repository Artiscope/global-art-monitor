Artist Market Profiles

Faith XLVII

Faith XLVII is a South African artist, born in 1979, who began writing graffiti as Faith47 in 1997 and has painted public murals in 39 countries. She also makes prints, hand-sewn hangings, bronzes and paintings.

Global Art Monitor has documented 2 auction lots by Faith XLVII, offered between 23 November 2020 and 10 June 2024 at Strauss & Co. The most recent on our record is Coltan; Oil; Diamonds, triptych, Strauss & Co, 10 June 2024, sold for ZAR 29,313 with buyer's premium. Source: Strauss & Co lot page.

About the artist

The table lists the two works on panel or found paper we could document, both sold by Strauss & Co in South Africa. Strauss states that its prices include buyer's premium and VAT, so they are shown in the premium column on that basis. The triptych did not sell when first offered on 15 April 2024 and sold two months later for ZAR 29,313 with fees, under its ZAR 30,000 low estimate.

Editions are tracked separately. Strauss sold Ex Animo (2017, screenprint, 21/47) for ZAR 9,369 on 13 July 2026, and two lithograph proofs, Omnia in Mensura et Numero et Pondere Disposuisti (ZAR 5,863) and CAPAX INFINITI (ZAR 5,277), on 25 October 2022.

Life and career

Faith XLVII was born in Cape Town and sprayed her first wall at the old Clovelly train station, according to Daily Maverick. She was among the most prolific names on the South African scene in the late 1990s and early 2000s, and by 2020 had been based in Los Angeles for three years.

Her murals now cover walls in 39 countries, according to Strauss & Co. In 2025 the STRAAT Museum in Amsterdam gave her a show and added a work to its collection.

Why the work is valued

In 2025 the STRAAT Museum in Amsterdam showed ORIGINS: Family & Legacy, from 18 April to 8 June, with work by Faith XLVII, her son and another artist, and added one of her works to its collection. Artdaily reports that her work has also been shown at the Brooklyn Museum.

Her murals stay where they are painted and do not come to auction. Her auction record so far is in South Africa and is small.
