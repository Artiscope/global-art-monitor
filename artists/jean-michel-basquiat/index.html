Artist Market Profiles

Jean-Michel Basquiat

Jean-Michel Basquiat was born in Brooklyn on 22 December 1960 and died in New York on 12 August 1988, aged 27. He first became known for the SAMO© graffiti he wrote around downtown Manhattan in the late 1970s, and by 1980 was painting on canvas. His working life lasted less than a decade, which is the main fact behind his market.

Global Art Monitor has documented 5 auction lots by Jean-Michel Basquiat, offered between 26 June 2025 and 19 November 2026 at Christie's and Phillips. The most recent on our record is Untitled (Leonardo and His Five Grotesque Heads) (1983), Christie's, 19 November 2026, sold for US$32,880,000 with buyer's premium (our calculation from the hammer price). Source: The Art Newspaper.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first. The newest is the top lot of Christie's 21st Century Evening Sale in New York on 19 November 2026. The other four sold at Phillips in 2025.

Untitled (Leonardo and His Five Grotesque Heads), 1983, a painting about 2 metres square in oilstick and acrylic, was estimated at US$30 million to 50 million. The Art Newspaper reports that it was hammered to a telephone bidder at US$28 million, US$32.8 million with fees. Christie's New York rates from September 2026 turn that hammer into exactly US$32,880,000, shown as our calculation. We have not read Christie's own lot page, so the lot number is still to confirm.

The four Phillips results come from Phillips' own lot pages. They range from a small 1980 collage on paper at US$21,930 to MP, a 1984 painting with Xerox collage, at £4,888,000. Posterior View, from Anatomy is a screenprint from an edition of 18, so it is not comparable with the unique works. Phillips also lists Fleas, Flies, a small painted wooden object, as sold in its London day sale on 17 October 2025, but shows no price, so it is not in the table.

Life and career

Britannica says his mother was of Puerto Rican descent, that he spent 1974 to 1976 in Puerto Rico, and that he left school and home at 17. With Al Diaz and Shannon Dawson he wrote the SAMO© tags. His first formal exhibition was The Times Square Show in 1980.

The next two years moved fast. The Museum of Fine Arts, Boston dates his first international solo show to May 1981, at Emilio Mazzoli in Modena, his first New York solo show to March 1982 at Annina Nosei, and his first Los Angeles show to April 1982 with Larry Gagosian. That summer he showed at Documenta 7.

Britannica dates his friendship with Andy Warhol to 1983. His first solo museum show was at the Fruitmarket Gallery in Edinburgh in 1984. He died of a heroin overdose in his New York loft in 1988.

Why the work is valued

The Whitney Museum of American Art owns Hollywood Africans (1983) and LNAPRK (1982), and held a retrospective of his work from October 1992 to January 1994. At 21 he was among the youngest of the 182 artists at Documenta 7 in Kassel in 1982.

The Whitney notes that an untitled painting of 1982 sold for US$110.5 million in 2017, still the figure most often quoted for his work. Supply is fixed and museums hold many of the major canvases, so a large painting of 1981 to 1984 comes up only occasionally.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Hollywood Africans (1983)

Acrylic and oil stick on canvas, 213.5 x 213.4 cm. Whitney Museum of American Art

Painted during a long stay in Los Angeles. The Whitney identifies the three figures as Basquiat with the artists Rammellzee and Toxic, and reads words such as Sugar Cane, Tobacco and Gangsterism as a comment on the roles once open to Black actors in Hollywood. His birth date appears in the picture as 12, 22 and 60.

LNAPRK (1982)

Acrylic, oil, oilstick and marker on found paper on canvas and wood, with rope, 220.3 x 168.4 cm. Whitney Museum of American Art

Named after an amusement park outside Milan. The crown near the lower right is the mark he used as a graffiti writer. The Whitney notes that the exposed wooden crossbars, tied with twine, may have come from the street.

Reading the prices

A unique painting, a work on paper and a print from an edition of 18 can carry the same name and sell for amounts thousands of times apart. Compare like with like.

The table holds two results above £2 million and one above US$30 million, all for works of 1982 to 1984, the years museums most often show. Two of the large works sold at or near their low estimates. Five results are too few to describe a direction for the market.
