Artist Market Profiles

JR

JR is a French artist born in Paris in 1983 who pastes huge black-and-white photographic portraits onto buildings, according to the Brooklyn Museum. He works under his initials and sells photographs, lithographs and other studio works to pay for his public projects, according to his own website.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first, and all five were checked on the auction houses’ own pages. Two are large photographs from small editions sold at Artcurial in Paris, one is a unique Cibachrome print, and two are prints from editions of 200 and 250. MutualArt lists 1,764 JR lots in all, 1,282 of them prints, and 140 offered between January and November 2026.

Artcurial’s lot pages give a sold price without saying what it includes. Its conditions of sale for this auction set the buyer’s premium at 28% plus VAT, and both totals work back exactly to round hammer prices of €12,000 and €8,200 at that rate, so we show those as calculated. Tate Ward states its prices include the premium, and its lot pages give the premium as 25%, which gives hammers of £600 and £5,000. Chiswick says its £889 includes the premium but does not give the hammer.

Not everything sold. In the same Artcurial sale on 18 November 2026, a Face 2 Face portrait (estimate €20,000 to 30,000) and a Wrinkles of the City work in ink on wood (estimate €25,000 to 35,000) found no buyer. MutualArt also lists a large Wrinkles of the City print from Shanghai, estimated at €40,000 to 60,000, as unsold at Phillips in Paris on 21 October 2026. We have not checked that one on Phillips’ own site.

One more caution about the name. Auction databases file other people who signed their work “JR” under the same heading, from a 1911 pencil drawing to books from a Montmartre library. We matched every sale here to JR (born 1983) through the lot description.

Life and career

Born in Paris in 1983 to Eastern European and Tunisian immigrant parents, JR began as a graffiti writer and took up photography after finding a camera on the Paris Métro, according to the Brooklyn Museum. His early projects, Portrait of a Generation (2004 to 2006), Face 2 Face (2007) and Women Are Heroes (from 2008), were pasted up without commissions.

In 2011 he won the TED Prize, worth $100,000, and used it to launch the Inside Out Project, which by the Brooklyn Museum’s count had pasted more than 400,000 portraits in 141 countries. In June 2026 he covered the Pont-Neuf in Paris with a 120-metre cave of printed fabric, La Caverne du Pont-Neuf, according to Perrotin.

Why the work is valued

JR’s best known works cannot be bought. They are pasted on walls, rooftops and bridges and left to weather away. What reaches the market is studio work: photographs of those projects, collages and lithographs. His website says these sales are the main way he pays for his public projects.

His reputation rests on museum and public commissions rather than auction records. The Brooklyn Museum gave him a large solo show, JR: Chronicles, from October 2019, the Louvre commissioned him in 2016, and his film Faces Places with Agnès Varda was nominated for an Oscar in 2018, according to the Brooklyn Museum and Wikipedia. He is represented by Perrotin, Pace, Galleria Continua, Galeria Nara Roesler and Simon Studer Art, according to his website.

Reading the prices

Compare like with like. A unique Cibachrome print nearly four metres wide, a photograph from an edition of six and a lithograph from an edition of 250 are different objects, and their prices do not belong on one line.

Watch the basis. Artcurial’s totals include a premium of 28% plus VAT and Tate Ward’s include 25%. The Phnom Penh print looks well above its estimate at €16,032, but the hammer we worked back, €12,000, was exactly the low estimate. Five sales over about three months say nothing about a trend.

These figures are a record of past public sales. They are not a valuation and they are not advice. For how to read them, start with what an auction result actually tells collectors.
