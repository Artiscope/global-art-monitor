Artist Market Profiles

KAWS

KAWS is the American artist Brian Donnelly, born in 1974. He began by tagging walls in New Jersey and Manhattan and painting cartoon figures onto bus shelter adverts, and is known for COMPANION, a figure with crossed-out eyes, in paintings, sculpture, prints and toys.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent public sales of unique paintings, plus one sculpture from an edition of three, found at Phillips and Christie's. KAWS sells at many houses every week; Sotheby's and smaller houses were not checked. Christie's shows a "Price realised", which under its usual terms includes the buyer's premium. Phillips' "Sold For" figure is shown as the price with premium.

COMPANION (ORIGINALFAKE), 3 of 3, sold at Christie's London in June 2026 for GBP 228,600, below its low estimate of GBP 350,000. KILL SITE (2019), offered at Christie's New York, shows no published result on its lot page.

Editions are tracked separately. At Phillips in October 2026, the three-screenprint set NO REPLY sold for USD 12,900 and a printer's proof of YOU SHOULD KNOW I KNOW for USD 7,740. The same SHARE screenprint (edition 500) made GBP 3,072 at Christie's London online in September 2026 and USD 17,780 at a Christie's online sale in July 2026.

Life and career

Before the toys, KAWS painted his own figures over adverts in bus shelters. Untitled (Haring) of 1997 is one, painted on an existing advertising poster and first shown at a friend's restaurant gallery in Seattle, according to the Brooklyn Rail. In 2006 he made ORIGINALFAKE COMPANION for his shop in Tokyo.

His first sculpture-only show was at CAC Málaga in 2014, and in 2021 KAWS: WHAT PARTY at the Brooklyn Museum was his first museum show in New York, according to the Brooklyn Rail.

Why the work is valued

The Brooklyn Museum gave KAWS a survey, KAWS: WHAT PARTY, from 26 February to 5 September 2021, with more than 100 works from early graffiti drawings and altered adverts to large COMPANION sculptures.

His market is unusually wide. He sells paintings, sculpture, prints and toys, and his name appears at many auction houses every week. That makes it especially important to separate unique paintings from editions and collectibles when reading his prices.
