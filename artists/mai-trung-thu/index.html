Mai Trung Thứ

Mai Trung Thứ, often signed Mai Thu, was a Vietnamese painter born in 1906 near Hải Phòng who settled in France in 1937 and died in 1980, according to Wikipedia and Sotheby's. Apart from a few oils, almost all his work is ink and gouache on silk, showing women, children and daily life.

Global Art Monitor has documented 5 auction lots by Mai Trung Thứ, offered between 28 September 2026 and 7 November 2026 at Millon, Christie's and Sotheby's. The most recent on our record is La lecture (1976), Millon, 7 November 2026, hammer price €1,500. Source: MutualArt sale record (house page not checked).

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first: one ink drawing in Paris and four silk paintings sold in Hong Kong in late September 2026. Every figure here comes from MutualArt's sale records, read on 26 November 2026, unless we say we checked the auction house's own page.

The silk paintings all beat their estimates. Mélodie (1950), the largest at 50 x 60.8 cm, made HK$4,608,000 against HK$3.2 to 5.2 million at Sotheby's, and three small works made between HK$576,000 and HK$704,000. The Millon drawing is a hammer price at the low estimate of €1,500. The Hong Kong figures have no stated basis on MutualArt.

Two older sales from the same month are worth knowing about. FauveParis sold Cinq Générations (1971) on 12 September 2026 for €1,430,000, almost three times its top estimate of €500,000, and Aguttes sold two silk paintings on 9 September, according to MutualArt.

Life and career

He was the son of Mai Trung Cát, governor of Bắc Ninh province, according to Sotheby's. In Paris he also played the đàn bầu, a Vietnamese one-string instrument, and filmed Hồ Chí Minh's visit to Paris in 1946, according to Wikipedia.

He made his own gilded frames for his silk paintings, according to Aguttes. Phillips describes his work as known for its delicate linework and muted palette.

Why the work is valued

Mai Trung Thứ was in the first class of the École des Beaux-Arts de l'Indochine in Hanoi, from 1925 to 1930, according to Wikipedia; Aguttes says he was one of six successful candidates out of 266.

His auction record is Portrait de Mademoiselle Phuong (1930), an oil, which sold at Sotheby's Hong Kong on 18 April 2021 for US$3.1 million with fees, according to Nhân Dân.

Reading the prices

Paris and Hong Kong both sell his work, and prices in this table run from €1,500 in Paris to HK$4.6 million in Hong Kong. Compare like with like before reading anything into that.

Size matters a lot here. A silk painting of 9 x 26 cm and one of 50 x 60.8 cm are not the same proposition, even within one sale.
