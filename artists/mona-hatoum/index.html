Artist Market Profiles

Mona Hatoum

Mona Hatoum was born in Beirut in 1952 to a Palestinian family and has lived in London since 1975, when the outbreak of civil war in Lebanon stranded her there during a visit, according to Tate. She worked mainly in video and performance until 1988 and has made installations and sculpture since 1989 (same source).

About the artist

Her work comes to auction less often than most artists here, so our five most recent documented sales go back a full year, to November 2025. Two were at Millon in Paris in July 2026, one at Sotheby's in London and two at Bonhams in New York. Every figure here comes from MutualArt's sale records, read on 26 November 2026, unless we say we checked the auction house's own page.

The two Millon prices are hammer prices: €45,000 for KAPANCIK (2012), a steel and blown-glass sculpture, and €3,000 for a photograph of her 1985 performance Roadworks. The Sotheby's and Bonhams prices have no stated basis.

Her bronze ∞ appears twice: an artist's proof made £28,160 at Sotheby's in June 2026 and number one of the edition made $19,200 at Bonhams in November 2025. Having two sales of the same edition so close together is useful, though exchange rates and the different premiums at each house make a straight comparison hard.

Life and career

She studied at Beirut University College from 1970 to 1972, then at the Byam Shaw School of Art from 1975 to 1979 and the Slade from 1979 to 1981, according to Tate and the Praemium Imperiale.

Measures of Distance (1988) is a video built around images of her mother, according to Tate. Later works include Light Sentence (1992), Corps étranger (1994) and Map (1999), made of glass marbles, according to Tate and the Praemium Imperiale.

Why the work is valued

Hatoum was shortlisted for the Turner Prize in 1995, according to Tate, and Tate Modern gave her a retrospective in 2016, according to the Praemium Imperiale. She won the 10th Hiroshima Art Prize in 2017 and the Praemium Imperiale for sculpture, according to the Praemium Imperiale and Wikipedia.

Tate's pages on her cover works including Grater Divide (2002) and Hot Spot III (2009).

Reading the prices

Thin markets need longer windows. With only a handful of sales a year, five results span twelve months. That is still too few to call a trend.

Museum standing and auction prices do not always line up. Hatoum's reputation rests on installations that rarely, if ever, come to auction. The sculptures that do sell are smaller editions.

These figures are a record of past public sales. They are not a valuation and they are not advice. For how to read them, start with what an auction result actually tells collectors.
