Pablo Picasso

Pablo Picasso was born in Málaga on 25 October 1881 and died in Mougins, France, on 8 April 1973, aged 91. Britannica credits him, with Georges Braque, as a co-creator of Cubism. He worked for nearly 80 years as a painter, sculptor, printmaker and ceramicist, which is why his auction record runs from eight-figure paintings to plates and jugs sold in editions of several hundred.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first. Three paintings sold on one evening, 17 November 2026, in Christie's 20th Century Evening Sale in New York, and two editioned works sold at Phillips in Hong Kong on 30 September 2026.

The Christie's figures come from The Art Newspaper, which gave both hammer prices and totals with fees. Le Baiser, dated October 1969, was estimated at US$40 million to 60 million and was hammered at US$26.5 million. Tête de femme (Dora Maar), painted on 25 May 1941, was hammered at US$25.5 million. A 1939 to 1940 painting combining Dora Maar and Marie-Thérèse Walter was hammered at US$6 million against US$10 million to 15 million. The Art Newspaper does not give its title, so we show it as to confirm. Christie's New York rates from September 2026 turn each hammer into a total that agrees with the rounded figure the paper gives, and the table shows our calculated totals. We have not seen Christie's own lot pages for these three, so lot numbers and media are still to confirm.

The two Hong Kong lots were part of a single-owner group that Phillips titled The Property of an Important Connoisseur: Picasso's Forms of Fantasy. Pichet au vase is a Madoura ceramic jug, numbered 125 from an edition of 500. Têtes de Béliers is a lithograph, numbered 23 from an edition of 50. Two other lots from the same group also sold: Colombe sur lit de paille, an earthenware plate from an edition of 300, at HK$83,850, and Scène de tauromachie, a plate numbered 63 of 100, at HK$64,500. Phillips also lists Homme assis, a red felt-tip drawing of 1971, as sold in London on 17 October 2026 for £83,850, but the lot page we read still showed the pre-sale view, so we leave it out of the table until it can be checked.

Life and career

His father, José Ruiz Blasco, taught drawing, and the family's moves followed his posts: to A Coruña in 1891 and to Barcelona in 1895, where Picasso entered the art school known as La Llotja. In 1897 he went to the Royal Academy of San Fernando in Madrid, found the teaching poor and spent his time in the Prado instead, according to Britannica.

He first went to Paris in October 1900 with his friend Carles Casagemas. Casagemas's death soon after is linked by Britannica to the paintings of the so-called Blue Period. By the end of 1901 he had dropped his father's surname and signed simply Picasso.

Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, unveiled in his Paris studio after months of revision, broke the figure into flat, splintered planes, with faces that MoMA relates to Iberian sculpture and African masks. With Braque he went on to develop Cubism. Guernica followed in 1937. Later he made ceramics in numbered editions stamped Madoura, which are among the Picassos that reach auction most often.

Why the work is valued

The Museum of Modern Art in New York owns Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, which Picasso began sketching in the winter of 1906 to 1907. The Museo Reina Sofía in Madrid holds Guernica, painted in Paris between 1 May and 4 June 1937 for the Spanish Pavilion at that year's International Exposition and returned to Spain in 1981.

His output was so large, and so varied, that a Picasso price means little until you know what kind of object it belongs to. A ceramic from a Madoura edition and a portrait of Dora Maar are both by Picasso. They are not in the same market.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Les Demoiselles d'Avignon (1907)

Oil on canvas. The Museum of Modern Art, New York

Five nude women built from flat, broken planes. MoMA notes that the title refers to a street in Barcelona and connects the two figures on the right to Picasso's visit to the ethnographic museum at the Trocadéro. It will never come to market, but it is the reason his early work carries the weight it does.

Guernica (1937)

Oil on canvas, 349.3 x 776.6 cm. Museo Reina Sofía, Madrid

Painted in Paris between 1 May and 4 June 1937, after news of the bombing of the Basque town reached him through press photographs. The Reina Sofía describes it as a general plea against the barbarity and terror of war rather than a picture of one event.

Madoura ceramics

Earthenware plates, jugs and vases in numbered editions

Stamped Madoura and catalogued by Alain Ramié, whose numbers appear in lot titles. The four sold at Phillips Hong Kong in September 2026 came from editions of 100 to 500 and made HK$64,500 to HK$206,400 by Phillips' results list. They sit in a different market from his paintings.

Reading the prices

Three of the five results in the table are hammer prices reported by the press, with totals we calculated from Christie's published rates. The other two are Phillips' own figures, which include the buyer's premium. Keep the two kinds apart when you compare them.

Two of the three Christie's paintings sold well below their estimates. That says something about those works on that night, with those estimates. It is not enough to describe a trend in the market for Picasso paintings.
