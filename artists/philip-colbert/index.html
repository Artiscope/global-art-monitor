Philip Colbert

Philip Colbert was born in Scotland in 1979 and lives in London. He studied philosophy at the University of St Andrews and ran a clothing label before painting full time. His large paintings are led by a cartoon lobster, which he calls his alter ego.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first, all at Phillips and all of unique paintings. Prices are Phillips' own and include the buyer's premium. The most recent is an untitled acrylic of 2021, one metre square, which sold in a London online sale that closed on 17 March 2026 for £7,095, inside its estimate.

The highest result we found is £71,820 for The Painter Portrait 2022 (Sky Blue), a 2.5 metre canvas, in Phillips' New Now sale in London on 8 December 2022, against £35,000 to 60,000. A Flower Study almost three metres square sold for £33,020 in July 2025 against an estimate of £6,000 to 8,000.

Phillips also offered The Coral Lobster (2025) in October 2025, estimate on request, sold to benefit The Coral Collective. The lot page shows no price. We have checked Phillips' archive only, so sales at other houses may be missing.

Life and career

After graduating he set up what he described to Hypebeast as an art-inspired clothing brand, and built the work commercially before painting full time. He has since worked with COMME des GARÇONS and Rolex.

The lobster arrived as a stand-in for the artist. I became an artist when I became a Lobster, he told the Saatchi Gallery. The Hunt Paintings borrowed the composition of Old Master hunting scenes by Rubens and Van Dyck and filled them with lobsters, hot dogs and cartoon sound effects.

Why the work is valued

The Saatchi Gallery in London has shown him several times: New Paintings in 2017, Hunt Paintings from December 2018 into January 2019, and Lobsteropolis in May and June 2021, which Saatchi called his largest UK survey to that date. His CV, published by Pearl Lam Galleries, lists group shows at Tate Modern and the Van Gogh Museum in 2015, and a solo show at the Wardlaw Museum in St Andrews in 2021.

Much of this record comes from galleries that show or sell his work. We found no museum collection holding a painting by him in the sources we read.

Reading the prices

All five results are unique paintings, so they can be compared, but size varies from 50 cm to almost 3 metres. Price per square metre is not a good guide either, since the largest canvas did not make the highest price.

All five come from Phillips' online, New Now and INTERSECT sales, not its evening sales. Five results over four years are too few to describe a direction.

These figures are a record of past public sales. They are not a valuation and they are not advice. For how to read them, start with what an auction result actually tells collectors.
