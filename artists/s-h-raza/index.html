S.H. Raza

S.H. Raza (1922 to 2016) was an Indian painter who co-founded the Bombay Progressive Artists' Group in 1947 and lived in France from 1950 until about 2010. His early landscapes gave way, from 1980, to geometric paintings built around the bindu, a central black dot.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent public sales of unique works we could document, all from September 2026 and all checked against the auction houses' own lot pages. Prices are in Indian rupees. Saffronart states that its prices include the buyer's premium. AstaGuru labels its price for La Provence Noire "inclusive of margin" and does not use the term buyer's premium; we show it in the premium column on that basis.

Earlier 2026 results are not in the table, for example Origin (2000), sold by AstaGuru in August 2026 for INR 47,158,954 inclusive of margin. A Raza lithograph on StoryLTD showed only a current bid when we checked, so it is not included.

Life and career

Raza was born in 1922 in what is now Madhya Pradesh, the son of a forest ranger, moved to Bombay in 1943 and studied at the Sir J.J. School of Art, according to Christie's. In 1950 he took up a scholarship at the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. He met the gallerist Lara Vincy in 1955 and won the Prix de la Critique in 1956, according to the Centre Pompidou.

He lived in France for about 60 years and taught at the University of California, Berkeley, in 1962, where he encountered Mark Rothko's painting. After the death of his wife, the French artist Janine Mongillat, he returned to India, according to Christie's. The bindu became systematic in his work from the 1980s.

Why the work is valued

He received the Padma Shri in 1981, the Padma Bhushan in 2007 and the Padma Vibhushan in 2013, and France made him a Commandeur of the Légion d'honneur in 2015. In 2023 the Centre Pompidou in Paris held a retrospective of more than 90 works, which The Art Newspaper described as his first in a state-run institution.

His market is centred on Indian auction houses, which is why most of the prices here are in rupees. His French landscapes and his later bindu paintings are different bodies of work, so compare prices within one period.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Saurashtra (1983)

Acrylic on canvas, 200 x 200 cm

One of his largest paintings. Christie's sold it in London on 10 June 2010 for £2,393,250, and The Art Newspaper reports it was bought by the Kiran Nadar Museum of Art.

Tapovan (1972)

Oil on canvas

Sold at Christie's New York on 21 March 2018 for US$4,452,500, which Christie's gives as his auction record. Christie's lists other results above US$1 million for paintings of the 1960s and 1970s, such as Village en Fête (1964) and La Terre (1973).

Bindu paintings (from 1980)

Acrylic on canvas

Geometric works built round a black dot. They exist in many sizes, so size is the first thing to compare.

Reading the prices

His prices are recorded in rupees, pounds and US dollars because he sells in Mumbai, Delhi, London and New York. We never convert them. Compare a rupee result with other rupee results, and keep the French landscapes, the dark 1960s and 1970s paintings and the bindu works apart.
