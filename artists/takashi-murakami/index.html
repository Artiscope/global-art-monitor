Artist Market Profiles

Takashi Murakami

Takashi Murakami is a Japanese artist born in Tokyo in 1962 who trained in Nihonga, the traditional Japanese painting style, and named his own flat, cartoon-bright style Superflat, according to Wikipedia and Ocula. His market runs from multi-million dollar paintings to screenprints that sell for a few hundred dollars.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first. They span almost the whole range of his market in just over a week: a large gold-leaf painting at Christie's in New York, a 2001 canvas at Phillips, a sculpture pair from an edition of 20 in Hong Kong, and two prints. Every figure here comes from MutualArt's sale records, read on 26 November 2026, unless we say we checked the auction house's own page. We checked the Phillips result on Phillips' own lot page.

Most of these prices do not say what they include. Phillips gives a 'Sold For' figure and the major houses usually quote prices with the buyer's premium, but MutualArt does not state the basis for the Christie's, Sotheby's or Seoul Auction lots, so we show them as From MutualArt with the premium not stated. The Showplace print is the only one where MutualArt says the price excludes the premium, so that figure is a hammer price.

Be careful with Murakami prints. Auction databases are full of offset posters sold as 'After Takashi Murakami', usually from editions of 300 and often for under €200. We left those out. The two prints in our table are a screenprint from an edition of 100 and a small numbered silkscreen.

Life and career

Murakami earned a doctorate in Nihonga at Tokyo University of the Arts in 1993, according to Ocula. In 1996 he set up the Hiropon Factory, a workshop that became the company Kaikai Kiki in 2001, according to Wikipedia. His studio is in Miyoshi, according to Christie's.

From 2002 he designed for Louis Vuitton at Marc Jacobs' invitation, and in 2007 he made the cover of Kanye West's album Graduation, according to Wikipedia. After the 2011 earthquake and tsunami in Japan he painted The 500 Arhats, more than 100 metres long, according to Ocula.

Why the work is valued

Murakami published his Superflat theory in 2000, in the catalogue of a show he curated at the Museum of Contemporary Art in Los Angeles, according to Wikipedia. In 2010 he became the first Japanese artist to show at the Palace of Versailles, according to Wikipedia and Christie's.

His highest auction price is still My Lonesome Cowboy (1998), a fibreglass sculpture that sold for $15.2 million in 2008, according to Christie's. MoMA owns his painting 727 (1996), according to Wikipedia.

Reading the prices

Check what you are looking at. The Christie's painting and the Phillips canvas are unique works. The Hong Kong sculpture is one of 20, and the prints come in editions of 100 or more. Their prices belong in different conversations.

Check the basis. Phillips' $193,500 for Splash Nude is the price with fees, and it landed just under the top estimate of $200,000. For the Christie's painting at $1,920,000 against an estimate of $1.5 to 2.5 million, we do not know from MutualArt whether the premium is included.
