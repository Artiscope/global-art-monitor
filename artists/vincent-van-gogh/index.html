Vincent van Gogh

Vincent van Gogh was a Dutch painter, born in Zundert in 1853, who died at Auvers-sur-Oise in France on 29 July 1890. In just over ten years he made about 2,100 works, around 860 of them oil paintings, most in the last two years of his life. He is known for bold colour and fast, visible brushwork.

About the artist

We have documented one sale so far, the auction record set in New York this November. Sotheby's sold Châtaigniers en fleurs (Chestnut Trees in Blossom), painted in Auvers on 24 May 1890, as lot 5 of the Blaquier-Arrieta collection on 18 November 2026. The estimate was given only on request. Art Insider reports US$190.4 million with fees and a hammer price of US$165 million. The Art Newspaper gives the same hammer price. Sotheby's shows the result only to signed-in users. Its published New York rates turn a US$165 million hammer into US$190,430,000, which agrees with the reported US$190.4 million, so the table shows that total as our calculation.

The previous record for the artist was US$117.2 million for Orchard with Cypresses (1888), sold at Christie's New York in 2022. The sale went ahead under an agreement between the seller and the heirs of Samuel and Hedwig Fischer, who owned the painting from 1909 until it was sold in 1936. Our report on the November 2026 New York sales, under Research Reports, sets it beside the other top lots of the week.

The Kröller-Müller Museum in Otterlo holds a smaller painting of the same subject, Blossoming Chestnut Trees, dated 22 to 23 May 1890. The museum describes flat strokes of green, short dark blue strokes giving the leaves structure and a sky of overlapping zigzag marks. Earlier auction results for Van Gogh will be added here once we have checked them.

Why the work is valued

Van Gogh had little success while he lived. His standing rests on more than a century of museum shows and scholarship since his death. Sotheby's lot page alone lists exhibitions of Châtaigniers en fleurs from Vollard's gallery in Paris in 1896 to the Fondation Louis Vuitton in 2026.

Very few major paintings by him come up for sale, and he made only about 70 oils in his weeks at Auvers. That scarcity is part of why one painting from May 1890 could make a price this high. It is not a guide to what his drawings or earlier paintings make.
