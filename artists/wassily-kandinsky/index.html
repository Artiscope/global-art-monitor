Wassily Kandinsky

Wassily Kandinsky was born in Moscow in 1866 and died at Neuilly-sur-Seine, near Paris, in 1944. He trained in law and economics before turning to painting at 30, moved to Munich in 1896 and from about 1908 began to strip recognisable things out of his paintings until only colour, line and shape were left. He taught at the Bauhaus in Germany until it closed in 1933.

About the artist

Two of the highest prices of autumn 2026 were for Kandinsky. At Christie's London on 14 October, Das bunte Leben (1907), a painting from his Munich years, sold for a hammer price of £27 million, £31,560,000 with fees, according to The Art Newspaper and London Art Walk. The Art Newspaper gives the high estimate as £25 million and My Modern Met gives £18 million to 25 million. Our table previously showed a high estimate of £35 million, which no source supports, and is now corrected. The lot number is still to confirm.

At Christie's New York on 17 November, Betonte Ecken (1922 to 1923) set his auction record. Christie's lot page, lot 12 A, gives a price realised of US$85,780,000 against US$60 million to 80 million, and The Art Newspaper gives the hammer price as US$74 million. Christie's New York rates from September 2026 turn that hammer into exactly the published total. The previous record, US$44.8 million for Murnau mit Kirche II (1910), was set at Sotheby's London in 2023.

Betonte Ecken has a museum past. Hilla Rebay acquired it in 1929 and gave it to the Guggenheim in 1941. The museum sold it at auction in London in 1964 for £22,000, and it sold again at Sotheby's New York in 1986 for US$1 million, according to Artsy and Art Insider. Our report on the November 2026 New York sales, under Research Reports, covers the rest of the week.

Life and career

Kandinsky thought of painting in musical terms. Between 1909 and 1914 he wrote four stage works he called colour-tone dramas, among them The Yellow Sound, none staged in his lifetime, and in 1913 he published Klänge (Sounds), a book of 56 woodcuts and prose poems, according to the Guggenheim Bilbao.

At the Bauhaus he published Point and Line to Plane in 1926, and in 1928 designed a stage version of Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition at the Friedrich Theater in Dessau, which the Guggenheim in New York recreated in 2012.

Why the work is valued

Solomon R. Guggenheim began collecting Kandinsky in 1929 and met him at the Bauhaus in Dessau the next year, and Tate showed paintings from the Guggenheim's collection of his work in London in 1958. In 1911 he founded the Blue Rider group with Franz Marc, and his 1912 book On the Spiritual in Art set out how colour could carry an artist's inner concerns to the viewer.

Tate divides his work into a freer, expressive phase from 1909 to 1918 and a tighter, geometric one from 1921 to 1935. Betonte Ecken comes from the second. Both 2026 results carry well-documented histories, which helps explain their prices.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Betonte Ecken (Accented Corners) (1922 to 1923)

Oil on canvas

From the start of his Bauhaus years. Once owned by the Guggenheim, it set his auction record at Christie's New York in November 2026. Museum provenance like this is part of what buyers pay for.

Several Circles (Einige Kreise) (1926)

Oil on canvas

Coloured circles on a dark ground, in the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum. Many of his key paintings are in museums and will not come up for sale.

Reading the prices

Oil paintings, watercolours, drawings and prints by Kandinsky are separate markets. Within the oils, the expressive years before 1914 and the geometric Bauhaus years are often compared, but each period is best compared with its own.
