Yayoi Kusama

Yayoi Kusama was a Japanese artist, born in Matsumoto in 1929, who died in Tokyo on 14 August 2026. She worked in New York from 1958 to 1973. She is best known for her Infinity Nets, canvases covered edge to edge in small looped brushmarks, and for her polka dots and mirror rooms.

Global Art Monitor has documented 5 auction lots by Yayoi Kusama, offered between 7 March 2026 and 16 November 2026 at Sotheby's, Christie's and Seoul Auction. The most recent on our record is No. 3P.B. (1960), Sotheby's, 16 November 2026, sold for US$15,055,000 with buyer's premium (our calculation from the hammer price). Source: The Art Newspaper.

About the artist

The table below lists the five most recent public sales of unique works that we could document, all from 2026. On 16 November 2026 Sotheby's sold No. 3P.B., an Infinity Net of 1960, in its Now and Contemporary Evening Auction in New York against an estimate of US$10 million to US$15 million. The Art Newspaper and Style Rave both give a hammer price of US$12.5 million, and Style Rave gives the lot number as 110. Sotheby's shows the total only to signed-in users. Its published rates (28% to US$2 million, 22% to US$8 million, 15% above) turn a US$12.5 million hammer into US$15,055,000, which agrees with HENI News (US$15.05 million) and The Art Newspaper (about US$15 million), so the table shows that total as our calculation. Style Rave's figure of US$14.7 million does not fit the rates and is not used. It is a new auction record for the artist, though the sources disagree on the record it replaced.

Christie's own lot pages now confirm the prices, estimates and lot numbers for Hitomi (Eye), Flash Towards the 21st Century and INFINITY-NETS (OOAXT). For Infinity Nets of 1970, sold at Seoul Auction on 31 March 2026, TheValue gives lot 39, a hammer price of KRW 2,000,000,000 and KRW 2,360,000,000 with fees. The estimate was given only on request.

Two other London sales from March 2026 fall just outside the five: Pumpkin (1991) at GBP 381,000 and Infinity Nets (1960) at about GBP 2.12 million, both at Christie's and both reported by HENI News.

Life and career

Kusama was born in Matsumoto in 1929 and studied painting in Kyoto. She moved to the United States in 1957, settled in New York in 1958 and came into contact there with Donald Judd, Andy Warhol, Joseph Cornell and Claes Oldenburg, according to Tate. Her first mirror installation, Kusama's Peep Show or Endless Love Show, dates from 1966.

She returned to Japan in 1973. From 1977 she lived by her own choice in a psychiatric hospital in Tokyo and kept working in a Tokyo studio, from nine till six according to Tate's 2012 film. She died in Tokyo on 14 August 2026, aged 97.

Why the work is valued

Kusama received the Praemium Imperiale for painting in 2006. Tate Modern showed her Infinity Mirror Rooms from 18 May 2021 to 28 April 2024, a run of almost three years. A museum devoted to her work, the Yayoi Kusama Museum, opened in Tokyo in 2017.

Her auction record, set in November 2026, is No. 3P.B., an Infinity Net painted in New York in 1960. Later paintings, Pumpkins and editions sell at very different levels, so compare each with its own kind.

Key works, and what reaches auction

Infinity Net paintings (from 1958)

Oil on canvas

Canvases covered in small looped marks, some up to 33 feet long by her own account. The New York nets of 1958 to 1973 are the rarest group, and her auction record is one of them, No. 3P.B. of 1960.

Pumpkin (from the 1980s onwards)

Paintings, sculpture and prints

Her most repeated motif. It exists as unique paintings, sculptures and large print editions, so a Pumpkin price only means something next to another Pumpkin of the same kind.

Infinity Mirrored Room: Filled with the Brilliance of Life (2011/2017)

Installation

Made for her 2012 Tate retrospective and now in Tate's collection. Mirror rooms rarely come to auction, so their value is mostly seen in museums, not in the saleroom.

Reading the prices

Three questions sort Kusama prices: when was it made, what kind of object is it, and is it unique? A New York net of the early 1960s, a Pumpkin canvas of the 1990s and a signed screenprint are not comparable, even at the same house in the same week.
