Artist Market Profiles

Zanele Muholi

Zanele Muholi is a South African artist born in Umlazi, Durban, in 1972, who describes themself as a visual activist rather than an artist, according to Britannica and Tate. Muholi's pronouns are they, them and their, according to Tate. Their photographs of Black lesbian, gay, trans and gender non-conforming South Africans are now regularly sold in New York and London.

About the artist

The table lists the five most recent sales we could document, newest first, all from October 2026. Four are gelatin silver prints from editions of 8, and one is a large oil painting, a newer part of Muholi's practice. Every figure here comes from MutualArt's sale records, read on 26 November 2026, unless we say we checked the auction house's own page. We checked the Phillips result on Phillips' own lot page.

Thembitshe, Parktown (2014), from the Somnyama Ngonyama self-portraits, made $28,380 with fees at Phillips, more than twice its top estimate of $12,000. The Christie's and Bonhams figures have no stated basis. All five sold within or above their estimates.

The painting Aphumelela (2021) made £16,900 at Bonhams, against £12,000 to 18,000. It is a reminder that a photographer's paintings can find buyers at similar levels to their prints.

Life and career

Muholi trained at the Market Photo Workshop in Johannesburg, according to Tate Etc, and earned a master's degree in documentary media at Ryerson University in Toronto in 2009, according to Britannica. They co-founded the Forum for the Empowerment of Women in 2002 and founded Inkanyiso in 2009 (same source).

Faces and Phases, begun in 2006, is a long series of portraits of Black lesbian, trans and gender non-conforming people, according to Britannica and Tate. Somnyama Ngonyama, which means Hail the Dark Lioness, began in 2014: self-portraits with props from scouring pads to tyres and cable ties, printed with the contrast raised to darken their skin (same sources).

Why the work is valued

Tate Modern gave Muholi a major exhibition from November 2020 to March 2021, and showed their work again from June 2024 to January 2025, according to Tate. They took part in the 58th Venice Biennale in 2019 and were shortlisted for the Deutsche Börse Photography Prize in 2015 (same source).

Their awards include the Prince Claus Award and the Carnegie Fine Prize in 2013 and the ICP Infinity Award in 2016, according to Tate.

Reading the prices

Editions of 8 behave like small runs. With so few prints, each sale says a lot, and the edition number (6 of 8, 8 of 8) is worth noting.

Museum shows move markets. Two Tate Modern exhibitions in five years have put Muholi in front of many new buyers, and the estimates at Christie's and Phillips reflect that.
