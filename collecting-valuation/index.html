Auction terms, in plain words

Hammer price
The winning bid, called when the auctioneer's hammer falls. It does not include fees. The seller's proceeds are worked out from it.
Buyer's premium
The fee an auction house charges the buyer on top of the hammer price. It is a percentage that varies by house and by price band.
Price with premium
The hammer price plus the buyer's premium. This is the figure most houses publish and most headlines quote. Also called the price realised.
Estimate
The low and high guide a house prints before the sale. It is a sales tool as well as a forecast, and is often set to attract bidding.
Reserve
The confidential minimum the seller will accept. By convention it is not set above the low estimate.
Bought in
A lot that fails to reach its reserve and goes unsold. Also called passed.
Sell-through rate
The share of lots offered that sell. A basic measure of demand for an artist or a sale.
Consignment
Handing a work to an auction house or dealer to sell on your behalf. The terms are set out in a consignment agreement.
Seller's commission
The fee the house takes from the seller, deducted from the hammer price. It is negotiable and rarely published.
Artist's Resale Right
A royalty due to living artists, and to their estates for 70 years, when a work is resold through the trade above a set threshold. In the UK it is charged on a sliding scale that starts at 4 per cent. Lots it applies to are usually marked ARR.
Primary market
The first sale of a work, usually by the artist's gallery at a set price.
Secondary market
Any later sale of a work, including auctions and private resales.
Liquidity
How easily a work can be sold, at a price near expectations, at a time of the owner's choosing.
Guarantee
A promise by the house or a third party that a lot will sell for at least an agreed sum. Guaranteed lots are marked in the catalogue.
Provenance
The ownership history of a work, from the studio to the present.
Fair market value
The price a willing buyer and a willing seller would agree, with neither under pressure and both properly informed. It is not the same as an insurance value or an auction record.
