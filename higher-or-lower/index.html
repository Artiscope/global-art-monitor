Higher or lower

Which one sold for more?

Two real lots from our auction records, sold in the same currency and of the same kind. Pick the one with the higher hammer price, then see both results and where they come from. Keep going for as long as you can.

How the game works

Each round shows two lots and asks which one made the higher hammer price: the winning bid when the hammer came down, before the buyer's premium was added. Get it right and your run goes on. One wrong call ends it.

We only set two lots against each other when the comparison is fair. Both were sold in the same currency, so nothing is converted. Both are the same kind of thing, two unique works or two prints and editions, never one of each. And both hammer prices were reached the same way: either the house published the hammer price, or the house published only the total and we worked the hammer back from it at the premium rates it published for that sale. The answer says which.

Every answer shows both hammer prices with a link to each source, and a label saying whether we checked it on the house's own page or took it from the press or a sales database. The game draws on 34 lots. Your best run stays on your own device.

These are records of past public sales. They are not valuations and not advice. For every figure with its source, see the auction results, and for the method, how we report a price.
