How to read the charts

A filled dot is the price with buyer's premium. A hollow dot means only the hammer price is known. A dashed ring is a figure from the MutualArt sale record that we have not yet matched to the house's own page. Lots with no confirmed price sit as small marks along the foot of the chart, so the gaps stay in view.

Dots are never joined up. Two sales are two different works, often of different sizes and made in different years, so a line between them would suggest a movement that the figures do not show. Where an artist has only a handful of priced sales in a currency, the chart says so.

Unique works are kept apart from prints, multiples and editions. A signed print and a painting by the same artist are different things and are not compared. The type of work is read from the description the house published. A painting or drawing counts as a unique work. Where the description does not settle it, the sale is marked as type not stated.

These are records of past public sales. They are not valuations and not advice. For the full method, see how we report a price, and for every figure with its source, the auction results table.
