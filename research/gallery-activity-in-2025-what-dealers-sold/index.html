Gallery Activity in 2025: What Dealers Sold, and Who Bought

By the Global Art Monitor research desk. 7 minute read.

Auctions publish their results lot by lot. Galleries mostly do not, so the clearest picture of what dealers sold comes once a year, in the Art Basel and UBS Global Art Market Report. This piece sets out what the 2026 edition found about dealers in 2025, and what those figures can and cannot tell a collector.

Where the figures come from

The report is written by the economist Clare McAndrew of Arts Economics and published by Art Basel and UBS. Its dealer figures are built largely from surveys of dealers, so they are estimates, not counted sales. Art Basel also runs art fairs, which makes it an interested party in any figure about fairs. We have taken every figure below from Art Basel's own summaries of the report, which are listed at the end.

The headline

Dealers, which in the report means galleries and other private sellers, sold an estimated US$34.8 billion of art and antiques in 2025, up 2% on 2024. Art Basel calls this a return to stability after two years of decline. Public auction sales grew faster, up 9% to US$20.7 billion. The whole market came to an estimated US$59.6 billion.

So galleries still sold more than auction houses by value, but their share of growth was smaller.

How dealers of different sizes did

The report groups dealers by turnover. Their results went in different directions.

Dealers by yearly turnoverChange in average sales, 2025
Under US$500,000Up by double digits (exact figure not given in the summary)
US$1 million to US$10 millionDown 1%
Over US$10 millionUp 3%, after two years of decline

Across all dealers, 42% reported higher sales than in 2024, 33% lower and 25% about the same. Costs rose too. Operating costs went up by about 5% on average, more than sales did, and 38% of dealers said they were less profitable.

Who bought

For collectors, the buyer figures are the most telling part of the report.

  • New buyers. Of the people who bought from a dealer in 2025, 49% had not bought from that dealer before, up from 44% in 2024. At the smallest dealers, with turnover under US$250,000, the share was 60%. At the largest, over US$10 million, it was 39%.
  • Fewer buyers each. The average dealer sold to 57 different buyers in the year, the lowest number since 2021. Dealers under US$250,000 sold to 29 on average, a fall of 40%.
  • Closer to home. More than half of dealers said higher taxes, duties and paperwork had put off some international sales. At the smallest dealers, local collectors made up 71% of sales to private buyers, up 9%. At the largest, the local share was 29%, up 6%.

Read together, these suggest smaller galleries were finding new buyers but selling to fewer people overall, and more of them nearby. That is our reading of the figures, not the report's.

Where dealers sold

Art fairs accounted for 35% of dealer sales in 2025, up 4% and the highest share since 2022. Most of that came at fairs abroad: 63% of fair sales were overseas and 37% at local fairs. Dealers with turnover of US$1 million to US$10 million leaned on fairs most, with their fair share rising from 29% to 36%. For the largest dealers it fell slightly, to 32%.

Online-only sales across the whole market came to US$9.2 billion, 15% of the total and the lowest share since 2019. The summary does not give a separate online figure for dealers.

What sold

The report's summary says sales by dealers in Old Masters rose 9% and by dealers in modern art 11% by value, while contemporary art dealers were flat. Works by women made up 37% of dealer sales by value, up from 28% in 2018. At galleries working mainly on the primary market, selling new work for living artists, women were half of the artists represented.

Openings and closures

Gallery closures make headlines. The report reviewed galleries that announced a significant change in 2025. Of those changes, 42% were openings, 25% closures and 33% relocations or other changes. Art Basel's summary says there was no evidence of closures outweighing openings. Just over half of the dealers surveyed had been in business for more than 20 years, according to Art Basel's article on gallery longevity.

That is a picture of 2025. It does not cover this year. The same Art Basel article notes that two established London galleries announced their closures in a single week in August 2026.

What dealers expect

Asked about 2026, 43% of dealers expected their sales to improve, 38% expected them to stay the same and 19% expected a fall. These are expectations, not forecasts, and we do not draw a trend from them.

What these figures can and cannot tell you

They describe the dealer sector as a whole. They say nothing about the price of any one artist's work, and a gallery's prices cannot be checked against them. A gallery price is a primary-market asking price set by the gallery. An auction result is a public price reached in a room. Our guide to comparing gallery prices with auction results explains why the two are not the same thing.
