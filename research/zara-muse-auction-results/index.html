Zara Muse Auction Results: A Documented Market Timeline

By the Global Art Monitor research desk. Published 23 November 2026. 6 minute read.

Zara Muse has a short public auction record: six priced results in a little over two years, at three London houses. This is what the record shows, and where it runs out.

The record so far

Each row below keeps three figures apart: the estimate the house published before the sale, the hammer price, and the total the buyer paid once the buyer's premium was added. All six sales were in pounds.

What the figures show

The first result came on 18 September 2024, when In Her Own Light (2023) sold at Tate Ward for a hammer price of £27,000 against an estimate of £6,000 to £8,000. With premium the buyer paid £33,750. Three months later The Shape of Stillness (2024) made a hammer price of £38,000 at the same house, against £8,000 to £12,000, for a total of £47,500.

The highest documented price is still the Bonhams sale of 3 April 2025. Malala Yousafzai, a portrait painted in 2019, sold for £51,200 with premium in the 20th/21st Century Art Day Sale at New Bond Street. Bonhams does not publish hammer prices, so we worked that one back from the total: about £40,000.

The next result was lower. Alya (2023) closed at £33,280 with premium in a Bonhams online sale on 18 June 2026, against an estimate of £7,000 to £10,000. Our calculated hammer price is £26,000.

Two paintings have since sold in online sales at Christie's in London, each against an estimate of £6,000 to £8,000. Ava (2025) made £44,450 with premium on 2 July 2026. Unspoken (2025) made £30,720 with premium on 21 October 2026. Christie's publishes the price realised, which includes the buyer's premium, and not the hammer price. We have now worked the hammer prices back from Christie's published rates: £35,000 for Ava, sold under the 27% rate, and £24,000 for Unspoken, sold after the rate rose to 28% on 1 September 2026. Both come out as round figures, which is a good sign the rates are right. Our guide to the buyer's premium shows the working.

How to read it

Three things stand out, and each needs a caution.

  • Every priced lot beat its estimate by a wide margin. Hammer prices came in at roughly three times the high estimate at Tate Ward and about two and a half times at Bonhams in 2026. At Christie's our calculated hammer prices were about four and a half times and three times the high estimate. That tells you bidders went well past the guide. It also tells you the guides were set low compared with where bidding ended.
  • The record has not risen in a straight line. In date order the totals run £33,750, £47,500, £51,200, £33,280, £44,450 and £30,720. These are six different paintings, of different sizes and subjects, sold in different kinds of sale. A lower result is not proof of a fall, and the earlier climb was not proof of a trend.
  • The sample is small. Six priced results cannot tell you what a seventh painting will make. They show what six works made on six days.

What we could not confirm

The hammer prices for Ava and Unspoken are our calculation from Christie's published rates, not figures Christie's has stated. We also have not confirmed the estimate Bonhams published for Malala Yousafzai.

A note on the 2025 record. Some reports give the Malala Yousafzai price in US dollars, at about $67,200. That is a conversion of the same £51,200 sale, not a separate result. We show every price in the currency of the sale.

If you hold a catalogue, invoice or saleroom notice that fills one of these gaps, tell us and we will update the timeline and credit the source.
